How it behaves

Marking a day read tears the page off: it lifts from the perforation, slides up and away, and the next day’s page is already underneath. With Reduce Motion it crossfades. Swiping sideways turns to the next or previous day. The torn stubs under the binding are the days already read; the edges under the page are the days still to come.

Printer’s blue belongs to the current day and the one primary action on each screen. Everything else is ink. Read and unread days differ by mark length, never by a second colour. No streaks, no scores; a gap is just the next page.

At xxxLarge the day number shrinks a step and the page scrolls; the verse moves below the fold rather than squeezing the reading.

Native notes

iPhone: tab bar for the four sections, the assistant and the paywall as sheets with swipe to dismiss, SF Pro for every control, SF Symbols for icons, Dynamic Type. Android: Material navigation bar, modal bottom sheets, system and predictive Back, Roboto for controls, Material Symbols, font scale, 48 dp targets. Both keep the same pages, numeral and serif.

Every control has at least a 44 pt (iOS) or 48 dp (Android) hit area, including the underlined text links. Ads appear only for free readers, as the one banner at the foot of Plan and other secondary screens, never on Today, the assistant or the paywall.

Paywall copy follows decisions already made: the subscription covers running costs, up to 30 questions a day, monthly and yearly, a yearly price review that passes savings on. Prices are not set.

Verses, Account, Settings, sign in

Verses keeps the page language: this week’s verse is a page on the pad with a Full text or First letters menu, practice reveals one verse at a time, “I know it” marks a verse learned, and the list runs week 1 to 52, opening at this week. More holds Timeline, Who’s who, Account, Subscription and Settings. Free readers see the banner on Plan, Verses and More.

Signing in and signing up are the same steps: Continue with Apple, Continue with Google, or an emailed code, no password. The app works without an account; signing in only keeps progress in step across devices and carries what is already marked. Apple requires Sign in with Apple next to Google on iPhone; Android shows Google (through the system account picker) and email only. Both stores require deleting an account inside the app. A reader who started without an account signs up later from More, Account. Deleting asks to confirm, clears this phone’s progress as well as the account’s (decided 2026-11-18), and says a subscription keeps billing until it is cancelled in the store.

Notes

Each day’s page has room for one note, the way a desk calendar page has ruled lines for notes: “Your note” and two ruled lines between the links and Mark day read, so the reader can jot something after reading and before tearing the page off. Tapping the lines opens a sheet with the day’s reading at the top and ruled paper to write on. It saves as she types (“Saved”); there is no save button. Done closes it, and the page shows the note’s first two lines.

Notes is a fifth tab, between Verses and More: every note, newest first, each headed by its day number, reading and weekday, with the first three lines; today’s note carries the current day’s blue. Search looks through the text. Tapping a note opens the same sheet for that day. In Plan, a day with a note says “Note”. Tapping any day in Plan turns the pad to that day’s page, note included; a day already read shows “Read on Tuesday” and a way back to today in place of the button, so blue stays with the current day. To delete a note: Delete note… at the foot of the writing sheet (when the keyboard is down), confirmed in a system alert, or swipe left on it in the Notes tab (iPhone; on Android, long-press for the same Delete). An emptied note is removed too. Plain text only; notes are free for everyone and never carry an ad. A search with no match says so in the list: No notes mention “manna”. The writing happens in a sheet rather than inline on the page so the keyboard never hides what she is writing and the page keeps its first-viewport shape.

Notes are kept on the phone and, once signed in, synced like progress, so the Account, sign-in and delete copy now say “progress and notes”. Delete account clears notes on this phone too. If the same day’s note is edited on two devices, the last edit wins, the same rule progress uses. The signed-in Notes tab has no sync line; Account already says everything is up to date, and the signed-out empty state is where the line earns its place. Proposed, to confirm: Reset progress keeps notes, because they are her writing, not her place in the plan.
